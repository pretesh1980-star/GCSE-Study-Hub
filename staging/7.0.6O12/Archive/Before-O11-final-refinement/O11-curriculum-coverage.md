# O11 Active Transport — curriculum coverage

Checked 15 October 2026 against the official [AQA Combined Science: Trilogy 8464 Biology specification](https://www.aqa.org.uk/subjects/science/gcse/science-8464/specification/biology-subject-content), section **4.1.3.3** and its transport-comparison links. This is a coverage review of O11, not certification of the whole Cell Biology course or independent written competence.

| Specification outcome | Teaching evidence | Independent check evidence, both fresh forms |
|---|---|---|
| Explain against-gradient movement | Stages 1–2: root puzzle, concentration labels and stepped membrane model | Definition item 1; transfer item 8; written response |
| Link transport to respiration energy | Stage 2: energy switch; active transfer fails without its energy supply, diffusion continues | Energy item 2; respiration item 9 |
| Explain mineral-ion uptake from dilute soil and its growth role | Stages 1 and 3; root example and ion/water distinction | Root item 4; form 1 written explanation |
| Explain glucose uptake when gut concentration is below blood, with its respiration role | Stage 3: overall gut-to-blood route, Stage 5 application | Gut item 5; form 2 writing |

| Apply evidence in a new context | Stage 5: supplied ion concentrations for a pond alga | Item 8: unfamiliar cell and numerical concentration evidence |

The core outcomes are shared by Foundation and Higher: section 4.1.3.3 does not mark them Higher-only. The same core lesson/check bank is available to both; records, seen IDs and scores remain separated by the existing board/tier/course context. Automated tier-switch tests verify isolation. The new interactive path is enabled only for AQA Trilogy; other boards/courses retain their existing pathways.

Membrane transport proteins are a short explanatory scaffold, not a claim that AQA requires detailed molecular pumps. Sodium co-transport, ATP cycles and membrane voltages are not assessed. The glucose drawing explicitly represents the overall route through intestinal cells into blood; it does not depict one direct gut-to-blood pump.

The comparison revisits 4.1.3.1 diffusion and 4.1.3.2 osmosis without replacing O7–O10. It retains AQA’s dilute/concentrated model and links it to water-potential terminology. Root structure links to 4.1.1.3; respiration is introduced briefly rather than turning O11 into a respiration lesson. Concentration is kept distinct from total particle amount through labelled equal-volume samples. Net transfer is distinguished from continuing random motion.

Existing project mapping remains unchanged: milestone 11, id `active`, specification 4.1.3.3, source subsection `Active transport and exchange` in cell-journey.js and biology-transport-completion.js. Original source notes remain available in the journey’s revision resources. Exchange-surface teaching stays with O8.

All new questions and written checklists are original GCSE-style learning material. None is labelled an authenticated past-paper item or official mark scheme. Written responses require self/teacher review; a structured score alone is not proof of independently explaining every outcome. No claim of complete course coverage is made.

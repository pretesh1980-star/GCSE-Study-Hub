# Build 7.0.6O4b4 — clearer microscope science explanation

Unpublished review candidate, 11 October 2026. Based on O4b3. No publication or new RP01.

The dense resolution explanation is replaced by a short three-picture sequence: two dots appear as one blur; enlarging that captured blur produces a bigger blur; a microscope with greater resolution can show the two points separately. The illustrations are schematic, not quantitative microscope simulations.

The terms are explained immediately in everyday language:

- Magnification: how much bigger; 100× means 100 times the real size.
- Resolution: how much detail you can see; the GCSE definition, distinguishing two close points as separate, is retained alongside it.
- Focus: adjust for a sharp image; it helps obtain the best view the instrument can give.

A simpler comparison explains what light and electron microscopes use, what each can show, and observing living versus conventionally prepared non-living specimens. The link between electron microscopy’s resolving power and discoveries of finer sub-cellular detail stays explicit. Higher useful magnification and resolving power are both retained.

Technical lens, vacuum and image-colour details, the equation, the fact that some mitochondria can be seen with suitable light microscopy, and focus/resolution limitations remain under Optional: a little more detail.

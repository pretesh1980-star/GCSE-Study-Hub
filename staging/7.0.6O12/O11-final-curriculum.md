## Science and answer keys

Each final form has eight items: definition, surface area versus energy, concentration diagram, roots, gut, two-answer comparison, osmosis transfer and respiration. The single-answer items have one scientifically correct option; comparison has exactly two. Distractors target lower→higher passive movement, osmosis of dissolved solutes, need reversing diffusion, area replacing energy, and all membrane movement requiring respiration.

Form 1 diagram: **2 inside → 6 outside**, active transport against the gradient. Form 2: **1 outside → 5 inside**, also against the gradient. Values, arrows, accessible descriptions and actual pre-submit render align with the respective question. No answer is written on the diagram.

Root-water questions explicitly state higher external water potential and against-gradient ion uptake. This removes the ambiguity of assuming root conditions from cell identity alone. Osmosis questions specify net water movement through a partially permeable membrane with appropriate concentration/water-potential conditions. Water potential remains a scaffold alongside the earlier dilute/concentrated explanation.

The gut diagram represents the overall route through intestinal cells into blood, not a direct one-pump connection or a claim that every membrane crossing is identical. Glucose crosses the intestinal lining; energy is ultimately supplied by respiration. No advanced co-transport mechanism or ATP detail is assessed. Food examples refer to starch and carbohydrate digestion without claiming all carbohydrate becomes glucose.

Reduced oxygen **can** reduce aerobic respiration and available energy; some respiration/uptake may continue. Slower active transport does not imply all passive transport stops. The gradient remains initially unchanged in the energy-problem challenge. The bicycle analogy explicitly represents concentration differences, not physical hills.
